# Little Explorers editorial proposal briefs

These five scopes are editorial briefs, not manuscripts, book records, or publication plans. HeavyVehicleBook is the only current pilot and has three complete bilingual draft stories in `catalog/kids-family.json`. The other four entries are intentionally concise proposals for a later phase. Their sample story titles and vocabulary are prompts for future editorial work, not commitments to produce those exact stories.

For all five proposals, the audience is a child reading with a caregiver; a suggested stage is a flexible starting lens, not an age gate. Later-stage links may use existing Learning Journey `revisits` only after a real story is reviewed and mapped to a few relevant concepts. No new Learning Journey IDs are introduced by these briefs.

## HeavyVehicleBook — editorial pilot

- **Audience and stage:** Shared read-aloud for approximately ages 3–5; preschool entry, with optional links along existing later-stage revisits.
- **Story seeds:** Three Trucks and the Garden Path; The Excavator and the Shape Garden; Big, Small, Up, and Down. All three have complete six-spread English and Korean drafts.
- **Useful vocabulary:** 덤프트럭 / dump truck; 굴착기 / excavator; 크레인 / crane; 굴러가다 / roll; 위·아래 / up·down; 크다·작다 / big·small.
- **Optional interaction:** Hide/show one caregiver question or reveal one picture detail. No correct answer, sound, countdown, or machine-control simulation.
- **Offline activity:** Print the bilingual spreads and use the color, shape, and size prompts for pointing or drawing on paper. No tools, construction materials, equipment imitation, or worksite visit is needed.
- **Fact and safety review:** The current limited notes cite NIOSH equipment visibility and backing guidance, OSHA construction vehicle and crane guidance, and FHWA compaction guidance. A later publication still needs a fresh source review and qualified safety/editorial review.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Read-aloud is complete without interaction; original SVG has localized scene descriptions; simple one-spread navigation and visible focus; children and visitors stay outside closed work zones; the story treats a shared garden path as ordinary community life without making abstract civic learning its main objective.
- **Progression:** Current themes start with `math-preschool-quantity-and-pattern`, then each adds only relevant existing strands: arts/computing, computing/arts, or science/computing. Stage choices follow the unique existing `revisits` chain for those exact IDs; no project-specific ages, gates, or curriculum claim are added.

## CarBook — proposal

- **Audience and stage:** Caregiver-shared stories for approximately ages 3–6; start with preschool movement, simple routes, and counting.
- **Story seeds:** “Where Does the Little Car Go?” follows a paper map from home to a garden; “Two Wheels and a Turn” notices a toy car's rolling wheels and left/right turns on a drawn track; “The Bus Has Room” explores different ways neighbors travel without ranking vehicles or families.
- **Useful vocabulary:** 바퀴 / wheel; 길 / road; 경로 / route; 멈추다 / stop; 방향 / direction; 함께 / together.
- **Optional interaction:** Reveal a drawn route marker or choose which illustrated stop comes next. Every route is printed in the story; no touch input or correct answer is needed.
- **Offline activity:** Draw a route between two picture places and move a paper counter along it on a table. Keep it away from real roads and parked vehicles; no driving role-play is required.
- **Fact and safety review:** Before drafting, check basic wheel/vehicle facts and age-appropriate pedestrian safety with current authoritative road-safety sources. Do not use a child as a driver, invite road crossing, or use real traffic lights as a game instruction.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Use captions/alt text for route drawings, multiple ways to describe travel, and varied family mobility; do not assume that every child rides in a private car or walks independently.
- **Progression:** Candidate starting strands are mathematics `math-preschool-quantity-and-pattern`, world history/geography `world-history-preschool-places-and-journeys`, and computing `computing-preschool-tools-and-cause`. These are not live links; editors should keep only concepts directly used in a finished story and follow only their existing revisits.

## DinosaurBook — proposal

- **Audience and stage:** Calm shared-read-aloud for approximately ages 4–7; preschool noticing with optional early-elementary evidence comparison.
- **Story seeds:** “A Shape in the Stone” notices a fossil imprint; “Big Footprint, Little Footprint” compares scale without claiming the print identifies a particular animal; “The Museum Question” lets a child ask how people know what lived long ago.
- **Useful vocabulary:** 화석 / fossil; 발자국 / footprint; 흔적 / trace; 오래전 / long ago; 같다·다르다 / alike·different; 증거 / evidence.
- **Optional interaction:** Reveal a second fossil clue or compare two illustrated shapes. The story names uncertainty and does not turn the interaction into a guessing test.
- **Offline activity:** Match printed fossil-like line drawings with their silhouettes or draw an imaginary trace on paper. Do not collect or break real fossils, rocks, or natural specimens.
- **Fact and safety review:** A future author must cite museum/university or peer-reviewed paleontology references, distinguish inference from evidence, and have a subject specialist review species, time, and scale claims. Avoid frightening injury scenes and false certainty.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Offer short, non-threatening scenes, shape descriptions in text, and varied dinosaur sizes and habitats; never imply that one picture represents all prehistoric life.
- **Progression:** Candidate starts are science `science-preschool-noticing-and-wonder`, mathematics `math-preschool-quantity-and-pattern`, and critical thinking `critical-preschool-questions-and-noticing`. These links require story-specific review before use; later links follow only the existing revisit threads.

## AnimalBook — proposal

- **Audience and stage:** Caregiver-shared stories for approximately ages 3–6; begin with familiar animals, body features, and habitats.
- **Story seeds:** “Who Left These Tracks?” notices signs without disturbing wildlife; “A Home in the Pond” explores a habitat through observation; “Rest, Move, and Grow” compares an animal's needs with a child's need for rest while keeping animal and human bodies distinct.
- **Useful vocabulary:** 동물 / animal; 서식지 / habitat; 발자국 / footprint; 관찰하다 / observe; 쉬다 / rest; 돌보다 / care.
- **Optional interaction:** Reveal a small animal in an illustration or choose which habitat picture to examine next. No sounds that scare animals or live-animal handling.
- **Offline activity:** Match printed animals to picture habitats or draw a safe-distance observation. Do not feed, touch, capture, or approach wildlife.
- **Fact and safety review:** Each story must cite current, species-appropriate sources from museums, universities, conservation agencies, or veterinary/public-health authorities. A reviewer checks habitat range, behavior, life cycle, and human/animal safety.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Include familiar and less familiar species, different environments, text descriptions for small creatures, and respectful language around disability and animal care.
- **Progression:** Candidate starts are science `science-preschool-noticing-and-wonder`, health `health-preschool-movement-and-rest`, and critical thinking `critical-preschool-questions-and-noticing`. The map should retain only concepts present in the reviewed story and follow their existing revisits.

## RobotBook — proposal

- **Audience and stage:** Caregiver-shared stories for approximately ages 4–7; start with simple tools, visible cause/effect, and imaginative design.
- **Story seeds:** “The Robot That Waited” notices a simple input and response; “Three Shapes for a Helpful Tool” sorts parts before a pretend design; “A Paper Robot Gets New Instructions” changes a short arrow sequence and predicts what the paper character will do.
- **Useful vocabulary:** 로봇 / robot; 기계 / machine; 움직임 / motion; 입력 / input; 반응 / response; 순서 / sequence.
- **Optional interaction:** Reveal one robot response after the caregiver reads the page, or select a short printed arrow sequence. The story works without tapping and explains that a machine does not think or feel like a person.
- **Offline activity:** Draw a paper robot and add two or three arrows for a caregiver to read aloud. No batteries, wires, magnets, small loose parts, or powered device are required.
- **Fact and safety review:** Check that any described sensor or response matches the represented device; distinguish fictional behavior from real capability; do not instruct children to open, wire, modify, or repair electronics.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Use predictable controls, clear cause/effect text and image descriptions, no sound-only clue, no flashing motion, and designs that welcome different ways of moving and communicating.
- **Progression:** Candidate starts are technology/computing `computing-preschool-tools-and-cause`, mathematics `math-preschool-quantity-and-pattern`, and arts `arts-preschool-materials-and-sound`. These remain editorial candidates until an authored story justifies the link; stage continuation uses existing revisits only.

## Shared review and release boundary

Every future manuscript needs English/Korean editorial review, sources and fact checks appropriate to its subject, caregiver/child-safety review, accessibility review, cultural inclusion review, and privacy review. Interactions remain optional and never grade or collect answers. An offline activity must be safe with ordinary paper and caregiver support. Passing those reviews would permit a separate decision about whether to commission a book; it would not itself make a title published.
