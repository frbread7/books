# CuriosityBook picture-book pilot specification

**Status: editorial pilot only — no authored book or published textbook exists.** CuriosityBook is a proposed Korean-first illustrated read-aloud series for children around ages 3–5 and their parents, caregivers, or educators. The central experience is a beautiful, enjoyable story shared together. Small optional interactions may add meaning to a moment in the story; they must never interrupt reading or be needed to understand it.

## Product premise

- **Parent Read-Aloud Mode is the default.** Each story opens in a calm reading view with large, legible Korean text, original illustrations, and simple page navigation. The adult reads aloud at the family's pace.
- A **Read without prompts** option presents the story without questions, tap targets, or activity interruptions. In the prompted version, a few naturally placed prompts may invite the adult and child to predict, notice, wonder, or talk. They do not have required answers and can always be skipped.
- A small story-relevant tap may enrich an illustration: tap a cloud to see rain begin, reveal a hidden animal, trace a shape, or hear/see a short optional detail. Taps must not gate the next page, award points, trigger timers, or take the reader away from the story.
- Digital reading is intentional and shared; screen reduction is not a blanket goal. Offline/printable editions are complementary ways to read, draw, talk, or explore, not a judgment about families who use screens.
- Korean is complete and primary. Optional English words, a short phrase, or a song may appear as a side note that never blocks the Korean story.

## Story and interaction contract

Each proposed picture book should include:

1. An original, coherent read-aloud narrative with a clear emotional arc, memorable characters, and natural page turns.
2. Commissioned or original illustrations that are culturally thoughtful, accessible, and licensed for reuse; do not copy Nuri, upstream textbook, or other copyrighted artwork.
3. A parent read-aloud layout with adjustable text size, strong contrast, keyboard and touch support, and meaningful image descriptions where they help. Illustrations must not carry essential information alone.
4. A small number of optional, story-relevant taps. The same story remains understandable with no taps, sound, animation, or account.
5. Sparse caregiver prompts positioned at natural pauses (for example: “What do you notice?”, “What might happen next?”, or “What are you wondering?”). The reader can hide every prompt and read uninterrupted.
6. A gentle after-story invitation to continue in ordinary life: look at rain from a safe place, count objects in a picture or at home, draw a shape, sing, move, or talk. Never require a report or screen-based result.
7. A printable/offline companion that stands on its own, including a low-ink version, adult safety notes where relevant, and accessible alternatives.

Avoid tests, quizzes with correctness feedback, competitive scoring, rankings, streaks, forced interaction, automatic audio, endless feeds, addictive rewards, unnecessary academic acceleration, or any collection of a child's name, birth date, image, school, location, profile, or progress. Do not add accounts, analytics, or public child profiles. Adults should be able to preview materials and safety notes before reading.

## Representative pilot story concepts

These are story premises for editorial development, not completed manuscripts or promised features.

| Working title | Narrative and learning texture | Optional in-story interaction | After-story possibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Where Does Rain Come From?** | Follow one raindrop through cloud, rain, stream, and return to the sky. Keep the water-cycle explanation accurate while using a warm, age-appropriate narrative. | Tap the illustrated cloud to begin a brief rain animation; reading continues normally without the tap. | Watch weather from a safe window or notice puddle shapes with an adult; no outdoor trip is required. |
| **How Many Friends Came to Play?** | An animal gathering lets the reader count, compare more/same/fewer, spot repeating patterns, and notice shapes in the setting. Counting is an invitation to look together, not a scored task. | Tap a group to highlight the animals or reveal a repeated pattern; never require tapping to count. | Count toys, plates, leaves, or steps together, or make a pattern with household objects. |
| **Meeting King Sejong** | An illustrated story introduces King Sejong and the historical context of the creation of Hunminjeongeum. Factual notes are distinguished from the fictional child narrator and any imagined dialogue; consult reliable Korean-history references and a Korean editorial reviewer. | Tap a large illustrated letter to reveal its shape or a short, optional observation prompt. | Look at Hangul signs or letter shapes together; no memorization or writing performance is expected. |

Further stories may explore art and music, movement and rest, community, place, and everyday science. Historical and cultural subjects require source review and respectful representation. Never present invented dialogue, characters, or events as historical fact.

## Curriculum relationship and editorial status

The Learning Journey's preschool entries are flexible concept invitations, not age targets or official achievement standards. The three sample story ideas can support Korean language/literature, mathematics, science, and Korean-history concepts. Other subject strands may be included only when an authored story genuinely serves them; a broad curriculum map does not obligate every story to cover every subject.

The editorial design references the 2019 Revised Nuri Curriculum's five integrated domains, research on early mathematics, NAEYC's developmentally appropriate practice guidance, and the National Academies' preschool curriculum report. These sources have different authority: Nuri is Korea's official curriculum for ages 3–5; NAEYC and National Academies publications provide recommendations and evidence, not Korean law; the specific narratives and interactions are original editorial proposals. Exact versions and review dates are listed in [Curriculum Sources](CURRICULUM_SOURCES.md). Use source facts and metadata; do not reproduce or adapt Nuri text, activities, or illustrations.

Activities should make room for child choice, conversation, rest, movement, repeated exploration, home languages, disability accommodations, varied family structures, and cultural context. Adults can model a question without requiring a correct answer. For the parent-child reading experience, a shared story is valuable in itself; the after-story real-world invitation is optional.

## Pilot review gates

Before CuriosityBook moves from specification to a separate textbook repository, prepare a small sample story set with original illustration rights, Korean-language editorial review, fact/source review for history and science, accessible reading and optional-interaction alternatives, caregiver safety review, cultural inclusion review, and privacy/security review. Test uninterrupted reading and prompted reading with adults and caregivers; do not collect identifiable child data. Keep the project `planned` until substantive content exists, its reviews pass, an independent site is deployed, and the Book Factory publication evidence contract is met.
